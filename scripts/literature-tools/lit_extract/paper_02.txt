Facilitated Self-Adjusting Mechanism with Mn2+ Additive in
Electrolyte for Ammonium-Ion Hybrid Supercapacitors
Zhenyun Zhao, Yunna Guo, Dongliang Chen, Xu Wang, Lei Deng, Yang Hou,
Qinghua Zhang, Zhizhen Ye, Liqiang Zhang,* and Jianguo Lu*
Ammonium-ion hybrid supercapacitors (AIHSCs) have gained extensive
attention due to their high safety and environmental friendliness. Manganese
oxides are among the most promising cathode materials; however, the side
electrochemical reactions occurring in aqueous electrolytes limit their
reversible capacities and energy densities. This work prepares the 𝜷-/𝜸-MnO2
electrode and reveals the side electrochemical reactions occurring in the
(NH4)2SO4 electrolyte. Besides the widely recognized dissolution of MnO2,
the re-deposition of MnO2 and irreversible insertion of NH4
+ exist
simultaneously during cycling, resulting in irreversible structural changes of
MnO2. A portion of 𝜷-/𝜸-MnO2 converts to 𝜹-MnO2, and a layer of
7Mn(OH)2·2MnSO4·H2O forms on the electrode surface, modifying the ionic
accessibility and structural stability of the electrode. The structural changes,
along with the competition among the three types of side reactions, cause
capacity decay and uprise during cycling. Accordingly, the self-adjusting
mechanism is proposed, and trace Mn2+ is added to the electrolyte to
facilitate this mechanism, thereby improving performance. Finally, the AIHSC,
featuring the MnO2 cathode and activated carbon anode in the Mn2+-added
(NH4)2SO4 electrolyte, shows 60.2 mAh g−1 at 0.5 A g−1 under 0–2 V. The
maximum energy and power densities of 60.2 Wh kg−1 and 5000 W kg−1 are
achieved.

light weight and high energy and power densities. Furthermore,
the high safety, mild pH, non-corrosive nature, environmental
friendliness, and abundant resources of ammonium salts will
expand their commercial applications in the future.[9] However,
they had not received much attention until Lukatskaya et al.[10]

The reports on AIHSCs have sharply increased since 2019,
and some cathode materials have been reported, including WO3,
MnO2, MoO3, VOx, Prussian blue,[9a,11] etc. Manganese oxides
(MnOx) have garnered signiﬁcant attention due to their ease
of preparation, wide resources and environmental friendliness.
However, undesirable side electrochemical reactions involving
the dissolution reactions of Mn species commonly occur in aque-
ous electrolytes,[8,11c,12], leading to low reversible capacities and
energy densities.
Some studies have proposed various strategies to address this
issue, including the ion intercalation,[11c] activation,[13] and va-
cancy engineering.[14] For example, Song et al.[13] deposited amor-
phous MnO2 on graphite foil and activated it in the CH3COONH4
electrolyte, obtaining 176.5 mAh g−1 at 0.5 A g−1 with a retention
of 94.7% after 10 000 cycles. To the best of our knowledge, this
presents the highest performance of MnOx electrodes in AIH-
SCs, whereas it just tested the single electrode performance in
a three-electrode measurement system, and the limited voltage
window (0–0.8 V vs Ag/AgCl) might restrict the energy density of
the corresponding full cells to some extent. Another key problem
is that most reports primarily focused on conﬁrming the build-
ing/breaking of hydrogen bonds with the insertion/extraction
of NH4
+,[12b,15] but the considerable side electrochemical reac-
tions involving Mn species in aqueous electrolytes, along with
the consequent eﬀects on electrode structures and electrochem-
ical performance, have been overlooked in previous studies of
manganese oxide-based AIHSCs.
This work prepares the 𝛽-/𝛾-MnO2 electrode and reveals the
side electrochemical reactions occurring in (NH4)2SO4 elec-
trolyte. Besides the widely recognized dissolution of MnO2, the
re-deposition of MnO2 and the irreversible insertion of NH4
+
exist simultaneously during cycling, resulting in the structural
changes of MnO2 after cycling. A portion of 𝛽-/𝛾-MnO2 converts
to 𝛿-MnO2, and a layer of 7Mn(OH)2·2MnSO4·H2O forms on the
electrode surface, modifying the ionic accessibility and structural
stability of the electrode. The structural changes, along with the
competition among the three types of reactions, cause capacity
decay and uprise during cycling. Accordingly, the self-adjusting
mechanism is proposed, and trace Mn2+ is added to the elec-
trolyte to facilitate this mechanism, thereby improving perfor-
mance. Ultimately, the assembled AIHSC, with the MnO2 cath-
ode and activated carbon (AC) anode in the Mn2+-added elec-
trolyte, can operate stably within a voltage window of 0≈2 V,
achieving high energy and power densities.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Structural Characterization
The original carbon cloth (CC) ﬁber has some micro-grooves
on its surface (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The MnO2-
deposited CC (CC/MnO2) shows a uniform MnO2 layer, with
a thickness of ≈10.2 μm, formed by tightly entangled MnO2
nanowires (each ≈10 nm in diameter), as shown in Figure 1a–c.
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is employed to charac-
terize the chemical compositions. The Mn, O, and C elements
are found in the survey spectrum (Figure S2, Supporting Infor-
mation). The C element is mainly from the calibration source
used during measurement instead of the CC substrate because
the detection depth of XPS is just several nanometers. The aver-
age oxidation state (AOS) of Mn in the deposited MnO2 can be
calculated via the separation distance (ΔEs) between the two ﬁt-
ted peaks of Mn 3s (Figure 1d),[14] and the relevant Equation (1)
is as follows:
AOS = 8.956 −1.126 ΔEs
(1)
Thus, the AOS is calculated to be 3.71, indicating the coex-
istence of Mn3+ and Mn4+. The high-resolution Mn 2p peak
(Figure 1e) is mainly composed of Mn 2p3/2 and Mn 2p1/2 peaks,
with Mn3+ (centered at 642.4 and 653.9 eV) and Mn4+ (centered
at 644.5 and 656.0 eV) both observed. The high-resolution O 1s
peak (Figure 1f) can be deconvoluted into three peaks centered at
529.8, 531.5, and 532.9 eV, assigned to Mn─O─Mn, Mn─O─H,
and H2O, respectively. Therefore, the chemical compositions in-
clude MnO2, HxMnO2, and interlayered/crystal H2O.[13] The cor-
responding structures can also be identiﬁed in the attenuated
total reﬂection Fourier-transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) spectrum
(see Figure S3, Supporting Information).
The XRD patterns of CC and CC/MnO2 are shown in
Figure 1g. Comparing the two, we can ﬁnd that the diﬀraction
peaks of MnO2 are located at 22.7°, 29.4°, 37.3°, 39.4°, 42.6°,
44.2°, 56.6°, and 66.6°, consistent with the 𝛽-MnO2 (PDF#24-
0735) and 𝛾-MnO2 (PDF#14-0644).[16] The HRTEM ware shown
in Figure 1h, with the selected-area electron diﬀraction (SAED)
given in the inset, and the partially magniﬁed images are shown
in Figure 1i. The nanowires with the diameter ranging from 2
to 10 nm can be observed from HRTEM, along with some ir-
regularly shaped nanosheets. The interplanar spacings of 0.211
and 0.241 nm correspond to the (300) and (131) planes of 𝛾-
MnO2, as well as the (111) and (101) planes of 𝛽-MnO2, respec-
tively. The SAED represents some diﬀraction rings, of which the
brightest ring is indicative of the (120) plane of 𝛾-MnO2. Thus,
the electrodeposited MnO2 is a mixture of 𝛽- and 𝛾-MnO2. Be-
sides, considerable amorphous regions are roughly visible from
the HRTEM, which can help the transport of electrolyte ions in
the bulk electrode, but on the other hand, they may worsen the
dissolution reaction of MnO2 and reduce the cycle stability.
2.2. Energy-Storage Performance and Self-Adjusting Mechanism
A three-electrode cell was utilized to test the electrochemical
performance. First, three types of ammonium salt electrolytes
were tried, and their GCD curves were shown in Figure S4

der of CH3COONH4 > (NH4)2SO4 > NH4Cl, while the poten-
tial window of CH3COONH4 is just ≈1.0 V, much lower than
the other two (1.2 V). Therefore, the (NH4)2SO4 electrolyte was
ultimately applied. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves at dif-
ferent scan rates (0.4–2.0 mV s−1, see Figure 2a) indicate that
CC/MnO2 can operate stably in the voltage window of 0.4–1.2V

For now, there have been few manganese oxide materials re-
ported for the ammonium-ion energy-storage devices, as sum-
marized in Table S1 (Supporting Information).[11c,12a,13,14,17,20]
The K-inserted 𝛿-MnO2 prepared by Krishnan et al.[11c] can oper-
ate under 0–1 V versus SCE with a speciﬁc capacity of 175 F g−1
at 1 A g−1, but it decays to 35 F g−1 at 5 A g−1 and only maintains
89% after 1000 cycles. Lu et al.[14] designed MnO2-x by an oxygen
defect strategy, which can operate in 0–0.8 V versus Ag/AgCl and
shows a high speciﬁc capacity of 109.9 mAh g−1 at 0.5 A g−1, but
it decreases to just 24 mAh g−1 after 1000 cycles. Song et al.[13]
prepared the MnOx electrode and proposed an activation strat-
egy to improve performance, which shows a speciﬁc capacitance
of 176 mAh g−1 at 0.5 A g−1 under 0–0.8 V versus Ag/AgCl with
a retention of 94.7% after 10 000 cycles. Herein, the CC/MnO2
shows obvious superiorities of high voltage window and long cy-
cle life, and admittedly, the speciﬁc capacity and rate performance
still need to be improved.
The observed trend of an initial decrease followed by ﬂuctu-
ations was generally noted in our experiments, and many Mn-
based materials reported elsewhere showed a similar tendency
as well.[11b,13,17,21] Thus, it is necessary to investigate the under-
lying reasons for this phenomenon via characterizing the struc-
tural changes of CC/MnO2 during the ﬁrst several cycles and af-
ter the activation. The illustration of voltage states is supplied
in Figure 2f, mainly including the open circuit potential (OCP),
half and fully charged in the 1st cycle (HC-1 and FC-1), fully dis-
charged in the 1st cycle (FD), fully charged in the 2nd cycle (FC-
2), fully charged and discharged after activation (Act-1.2 V and
Act-0.4 V). The ex situ XPS curves at diﬀerent voltage states in
diﬀerent cycling stages were recorded, with the O 1s and N 1s
peaks shown in Figure 2h,i, as well as the Mn 3s, Mn 2p, and S
2p peaks supplemented in Figure S7 (Supporting Information).
The OCP only contains Mn and O elements, but once it is charged
(HC-1 and FC-1), the N element appears, indicating that NH4
+
will insert to MnO2 in the 1st charge process (see Figure 2g and
Equation 2). Then, the N content continues to rise and almost
keeps stable in FD, FC-2, Act-1.2 V, and Act-0.4 V, signifying that
some NH4
+ would irreversibly insert to MnO2 during the ﬁrst
several cycles until reach stable (see Equations 2 and 5). Although
the insertion of cations during charging seems to contradict with
electrostatic forces, the strong hydrogen bonding force can drive
NH4
+ to insert to the near-surface of the electrode.[14] It should
be noted that a part of previously inserted NH4
+ will extract from
the electrode due to electrostatic repulsion during the 2nd charge
process (from FD to FC-2), but the inserted NH4
+ driven by hy-
drogen bonding force are still more than the extracted, thereby
overall increasing the N content. Comparing Act-1.2 V with Act-
0.4 V, we can see that the N content is higher in Act-0.4 V, demon-
strating the insertion and extraction of NH4
+ in discharge and
charge (Equation 12), respectively. There also exists the inser-
tion/extraction of H+ (Equation 13), which is reported elsewhere
as well.[22] The main reason is that the pH of 1 M (NH4)2SO4
is ≈5.13, and H+ has a smaller ionic size than NH4
+. The high-
resolution N 1s peak can be divided into two peaks (Figure 2h),
belonging to N+-H and N─H, and their ratio is tightly related
to the polarization of NH4
+ and the interaction between NH4
+
and MnO2.[11b,23] The ratio of N+-H to N─H gradually increases
in HC-1, FC-1, FD, and FC-2, demonstrating the inserted NH4
+
in the ﬁrst several cycles have relatively strong interaction with
MnO2. However, the ratio gets decreased in Act-1.2 and Act-0.4
V, indicating irreversible structural changes of the electrode after
activation.
The O content almost continuously decays from OCP to Act-
0.4 V (Figure 2g) because some interlayer/crystal H2O extract
from MnO2 to become free H2O (Equation 3) and a part of MnO2
was degraded (Equations 7–9).[17,24] From the high-resolution O
1s peaks (Figure 2i), the contents of Mn─O─H and H2O in FC-2
are obviously decreased than those in OCP, which are also at-
tributed to the irreversible extraction of H2O/H+ and the disso-
lution of MnO2. Some Mn─O-H may regenerate after activation
(Act-1.2 V and Act-0.4 V) and hence the Mn─O─H intensity re-
covers to some extent.
The Mn content decreases in FC-1 and FD but re-increases in
FC-2, and keeps stable in Act-1.2 V and Act-0.4 V (Figure 2g). This
is because MnO2 will dissolve and redeposit during discharge
and charge processes, respectively (Equations 7–11), which were
also observed in other manganese oxide-based aqueous batteries
or SCs.[22,25] Noticeably, HxMnO2 is not stable either, and hence
is easy to produce dissolvable Mn2+ via the reaction with H+
(Equations 8 and 9) and the disproportionation reaction (Equa-
tion 10).[24] The MnO2 structures get more stable after activa-
tion, thereby bringing about the almost unchanged Mn content
in Act-1.2 V and Act-0.4 V. From the high-resolution Mn 3s peaks
(Figure S7, Supporting Information), the AOS of Mn are calcu-
lated to be 3.71, 3.75, 3.66, and 3.75 in OCP, FC-1, FD, and FC-
2, respectively, conﬁrming that redox reactions occur in MnO2
during charging and discharging. The AOS turn to be 3.69 and
3.33 in Act-1.2 V and Act-0.4 V, respectively, which are lower than
those in the initial cycles because of the irreversible insertion of
some NH4
+ in MnO2 during activation. Besides, the AOS varia-
tion between charged and discharged states is minor in the ﬁrst
several cycles, whereas turns remarkably enhanced after activa-
tion, manifesting the larger accessibility of electrolyte ions in
electrodes and the irreversible structural changes of electrodes.
The Mn 2p spectra are also supplied in Figure S7 (Supporting
Information), corresponding with the Mn 3s spectra. Moreover,
a small amount of S occurs after activation (Figure 2g), and the

some NH4

the three types of side electrochemical reactions coexist during
charge-discharge cycling and compete with each other, resulting

As shown in Figure 3, the discharge and charge processes
are usually accompanied by the dissolution and redeposition of
MnO2 and the irreversible insertion of some NH4
+. These side
reactions compete with each other, making irreversible struc-
tural changes of MnO2 after cycling, mainly including the partial
phase conversion from 𝛽-/𝛾-MnO2 to 𝛿-MnO2 and the formation
of MSH layers on the electrode surface. They both have larger
interplanar spacings, and the MSH layers exhibit a protective ef-
fect as well, so the ionic accessibility in electrodes is enhanced
and the electrode structures become more stable in the later cy-
cles. Besides, the dissolution and redeposition of MnO2 cause ca-
pacity decay and uprise, respectively; the irreversible insertion of
NH4
+ shows both positive and negative eﬀects. Consequently, the
structural changes of MnO2, along with the competition among
the three types of side reactions, result in capacity decay and
rise during long cycling. In other words, these side electrochem-
ical reactions can modify the MnO2 electrode structures and its
electrochemical properties, which we deﬁne as the self-adjusting
mechanism. Accordingly, side electrochemical reactions are not
always detrimental; rather, they can enhance energy storage per-
formance if properly utilized. Then, we introduce trace amounts
of Mn2+ salts into the electrolyte to adjust the competitive bal-
ance of these side reactions and facilitate the self-adjusting mech-
anism to improve performance.

3. Conclusion
This work utilizes a simple electrodeposition method to prepare
the 𝛽-/𝛾-MnO2 electrode and explores their applications in AIH-
SCs. Besides the widely recognized dissolution of MnO2, the re-
deposition of MnO2 and the irreversible insertion of NH4
+ oc-
cur simultaneously during cycling, resulting in irreversible struc-
tural changes of MnO2 after cycling. A portion of 𝛽-/𝛾-MnO2
converts to 𝛿-MnO2, and a layer of 7Mn(OH)2·2MnSO4·H2O
forms on the electrode surface, modifying the ionic accessibility
and structural stability of the electrode. The structural changes,
along with the competition among the three types of reactions,
contribute to capacity decay and uprise during cycling. We ac-
cordingly propose the self-adjusting mechanism and facilitate it
through adding a trace amount of Mn2+ salts into the electrolyte
to improve electrochemical performance. The Mn2+ additive in
electrolytes can deposit extra active substances (𝛽-/𝛾-/𝛿-MnO2
and MSH mixtures) containing more 𝛿-MnO2/MSH on the elec-
trode, supplementing capacity and enhancing cycle stability. Con-
sequently, the assembled MnO2//AC AIHSC shows 60.2 mAh
g−1 at 0.5 A g−1 (3.96 mA cm−2) under 0–2 V, with the maximum
energy and power densities of 60.2 Wh kg−1 and 5000 W kg−1, re-
spectively. This work reveals the side electrochemical reactions of
MnO2 in the aqueous (NH4)2SO4 electrolyte, as well as their in-
ﬂuences on electrode structures and electrochemical properties.
Further investigations on side electrochemical reactions are nec-
essary to design high-performance and long-cycle AIHSCs in the
future.
