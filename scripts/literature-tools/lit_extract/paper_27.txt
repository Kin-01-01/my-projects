A High-Energy Aqueous Aluminum-Manganese Battery
Shiman He, Jie Wang, Xu Zhang, Jingzhao Chen, Zichun Wang, Tingting Yang, Zhiwei Liu,  
Yuan Liang, Boya Wang, Shiqi Liu, Liqiang Zhang, Jianyu Huang, Jun Huang,  
Luke A. O’Dell, and Haijun Yu*
Rechargeable aluminum-ion batteries have drawn considerable attention 
as a new energy storage system, but their applications are still significantly 
impeded by critical issues such as low energy density and the lack of excellent 
electrolytes. Herein, a high-energy aluminum-manganese battery is fabricated 
by using a Birnessite MnO2 cathode, which can be greatly optimized by 
a divalence manganese ions (Mn2+) electrolyte pre-addition strategy. The 
battery exhibits a remarkable energy density of 620 Wh kg−1 (based on the 
Birnessite MnO2 material) and a capacity retention above 320 mAh g−1 
for over 65 cycles, much superior to that with no Mn2+ pre-addition. The 
electrochemical reactions of the battery are scrutinized by a series of 
analysis techniques, indicating that the Birnessite MnO2 pristine cathode 
is first reduced as Mn2+ to dissolve in the electrolyte upon discharge, and 
AlxMn(1−x)O2 is then generated upon charge, serving as a reversible cathode 
active material in following cycles. This work provides new opportunities for 
the development of high-performance and low-cost aqueous aluminum-ion 
batteries for prospective applications.

Herein, a high-energy aqueous aluminum-manganese

employed as an efficient strategy to enhance the performance

320 mAh g−1 for 65 cycles. To understand the electrochem-
istry of this battery, a series of analysis techniques have been 
employed to investigate the components and structures of 
cathode materials at diverse discharge/charge states. It dem-
onstrates a conversion from Birnessite type MnO2 cathode to 
soluble Mn2+ in the electrolyte in the first discharge process, 
and the subsequent formation of AlxMn(1−x)O2 in the charge 
process, which serves as the reversible cathode active material 
of the battery in following cycles. Based on the reaction mech-
anism, the enhancement of AIBs by the Mn2+ pre-addition 
strategy can be well interpreted.
2. Results and Discussion
The Bir-MnO2 cathode was synthesized by our previously 
reported hydrothermal method.[21] It has a sheet-like mor-
phology as revealed by field emission scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM) (Figure  1a). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern 
of the as-prepared material demonstrates a Birnessite crystal 
structure (PDF No. 43-1456, space group C2/m),[10] with a 
characteristic diffraction peak at 12.4° corresponding to the 
(001) plane (interplanar spacing: 0.715 nm). The Na/Mn molar 
ratio of the Bir-MnO2 is ≈0.18 as analyzed by inductively cou-
pled plasma atomic emission spectrometer (ICP-AES), while 
the crystal H2O/MnO2 molar ratio is 0.525 as calculated from 
the weight loss (9.37%) of Bir-MnO2 between 110 and 250 °C 
by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) (Figure 1c).[10] Therefore, 
the chemical formula of the Bir-MnO2 is determined to be 
Na0.18MnO2·0.525H2O.
The morphology and crystal structure of the Bir-MnO2 
were further investigated by transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM). Figure  1d clearly shows the 2D morphology of a typ-
ical Bir-MnO2 sheet, with lateral sizes at sub-micrometer level 
and a thickness of around 10  nm. The selected area electron

The electrochemical performance of the Bir-MnO2 cathode 
was evaluated in aqueous AIBs, with 2 m aqueous aluminum 
trifluoromethanesulfonate (Al(OTF)3) solution as the electrolyte 
and Al foils as the anode in a coin cell configuration. Two kinds 
of Al foil anodes were compared, including the pristine Al foil 
(Al) and the Al foil after immersed in the aluminum chloride/1-
ethyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride (AlCl3/[EMIm]Cl) IL (T-Al). 
The first discharge profile of the AIBs is quite different from 
the following discharge profiles, suggesting the first discharge/
charge operation generates a new reversible cathode active mate-
rial, which will be discussed next. In the second cycle, although 
the Al and T-Al anodes can yield a close discharge capacity, the 
Al/Bir-MnO2 and T-Al/Bir-MnO2 batteries exhibit different 
discharge voltage plateaus of around 1.1 and 1.4  V versus 
Al3+/Al at 100 mA g−1, respectively (Figure 2a and Figure S2, 
Supporting Information). Figure S3 (Supporting Information) 
shows the potential gap between plating and stripping of Al in 
the battery with T-Al electrode is much lower than that of the 
battery with original Al electrode, in accordance with the galva-
nostatic discharge/charge curves. It demonstrates that the over-
potential of aqueous AIBs can be suppressed by treating the Al 
anode with IL, which can be attributed to the removal of the 
passive oxide film and the formation of artificial solid electro-
lyte interphases (ASEI) on the surface of Al anode.[20] It is prob-
able that such ASEI layers facilitated the plating and stripping 
of Al in this electrolyte. Therefore, the T-Al foil was adopted as 
the superior anode in the following studies. The corresponding 
AIB can exhibit a high reversible capacity of ≈350 mAh g−1  
as shown in Figure 2a, a relatively high value among reported 
AIBs. Unfortunately, this battery suffers from a fast capacity 
decay from 350 to 175 mAh g−1 within only ten cycles 
(Figure S4, Supporting Information). The X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS) investigation on the separator after battery 
operation shows noticeable Mn 2p3/2 signals (Figure S5, Sup-
porting Information), suggesting Mn species of the cathode 
can dissolve into the electrolyte, leading to the severe capacity 
fading. Moreover, the Mn 2p3/2 peak of this separator is con-
sistent with that of raw MnSO4, revealing that the Bir-MnO2 
was dissolved as Mn2+ into the electrolyte during cycling 
(more details have been discussed in Figure S5, Supporting 
Information).

The T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 battery could deliver revers-
ible specific capacities of 530, 315, 221, and 125 mAh g−1 at 
different current density of 100, 150, 200, and 300  mA g−1 
(Figure  2c), respectively, displaying a better rate performance 
than that of the batteries with 0, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.6 m MnSO4 
additive (Figure S11, Supporting Information). The reset 
of current density at 100  mA g−1 led to the recovery of the 
capacity to a level of ≈356 mAh g−1. As to the cycle stability 
in Figure  2d, the T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 battery displays a 
promising cycle performance of ≈320 mAh g−1 for 65 cycles, 
while the T-Al/Bir-MnO2 battery shows a rapid capacity decay 
to only 42 mAh g−1 in 30 cycles. The T-Al/0.3Mn/Bir-MnO2 
and T-Al/0.4Mn/Bir-MnO2 batteries can deliver 141 and 
269 mAh g−1 after 65 cycles, respectively (Figure S12, Sup-
porting Information). However, the T-Al/0.6Mn/Bir-MnO2 bat-
tery delivers only ≈50 mAh g−1 after 50 cycles due to the excess 
MnSO4. In all, the Mn pre-addition strategy can efficiently 
enhance the capacity, rate performance, and long-cycle stability 
of our aqueous AIBs.
The electrochemical performance of our aqueous AIBs 
is compared with that of reported AIBs. Figure  2e shows 
the plots of output voltages (the highest voltage plateau of 
the discharge curve) versus the specific capacities (based on the 
active material) of representative cathode materials in both 
nonaqueous and aqueous AIBs. Compared with the reported 
aqueous Al/MnO2 batteries,[14,20] and nonaqueous AIBs using 
vanadium compounds[5,22–25] or transition metal sulfides 
(TMS) as cathodes,[7,13,26–33] our T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 bat-
tery is superior in the capacity, working voltage and thus 
energy density. Although Al/carbon batteries using ILs or 
molten salt electrolytes have higher working voltages than 
the T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 battery, their specific capacities are 
significantly lower, resulting in lower energy densities.[6,11,34–37] 
Moreover, nonaqueous rechargeable Al-S batteries have shown 
higher energy densities because of the high capacity of S, but 
still suffer from sluggish kinetics and severe capacity decay, not 
to mention the high-cost electrolyte or the sensitivity to ambient 
atmosphere.[12,38–41] Therefore, the T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 bat-
tery is a promising battery system in the AIBs family.

T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 battery, a series of mechanism studies 
using ex situ XRD, ex situ Raman, XPS, and SEM have been 
performed in this work. The cathode materials at various dis-
charged/charged states (as marked by states A–I in Figure 3a) 
were collected for these measurements. The ex situ XRD 
results in Figure 3b show that the characteristic Birnessite (001) 
peak at 12.4° faded during the first discharge processes (A–C), 
and totally disappeared in the fully discharged state (D) and 
the following states (E–I). It suggests that the Birnessite struc-
ture of Bir-MnO2 undergoes disintegration upon discharge/
charge processes, which is very different from other Mn-based 
cathodes.[9,42] Such a conversion is irreversible as no diffraction 
peaks of Bir-MnO2 can appear in the 15th cycle (Figure S13, 
Supporting Information). To avoid the influence of electrode’s 
washing process on the XRD characterization, unwashed elec-
trodes after discharge/charge processes were also examined, 
and no peaks except for those from the electrolyte could be 
observed (Figure S14, Supporting Information), further demon-
strating the irreversible conversion of Bir-MnO2 into noncrys-
talline materials.
Ex situ Raman spectra of washed cathodes at various states 
were also employed to further study the reaction mechanism. 
As seen in Figure 3c, the Raman spectrum of pristine Bir-MnO2 
shows a main peak located at 579 cm−1, as well as several weak 
peaks at 513, 480, and 405 cm−1. The main peak can be attrib-
uted to the ν3(MnO) stretching vibration in the MnO6 unit.[43] 
Upon discharging, the ν3 vibration was gradually weakened 
and finally disappeared (point D), and the other peaks can no 
longer be seen. The Raman spectrum of the unwashed cathode 
gives the same conclusion, although some signals from the 
electrolyte can be observed (Figure S15, Supporting Informa-
tion). It demonstrates that the Bir-MnO2 undergoes a structural 
conversion, to new materials in the cathode with no MnO 
stretching vibration signals when fully discharged. However, 
the peaks (located at 505 and 575 cm−1) emerge again with a 
slight redshift from the charge state E to the discharged state 
H in the second cycle, while other peaks of Bir-MnO2 cannot 
be observed. It indicates that the charged product probably 
contains MnO bonds but is no longer Bir-MnO2. In addition, 
the spectra of fully discharged and charged states in the 15th 
cycle are the same as those of fully discharged state D and fully 
charged state F in the first cycle (Figure S16, Supporting Infor-
mation). The reversible emerging and vanishing of this peak, 
after the fully discharge process of first cycle, unambiguously 
verify the reversible reaction of the T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 bat-
tery once the Bir-MnO2 was discharged.

at 654.2 and 642.7 eV, respectively,[44] which disappeared in the

Based on the above analysis, it is clear that Bir-MnO2 under-
goes dissolution into the electrolyte during the first discharge 
process, while a new Mn compound is formed during the first 
charge process and serves as the reversible cathode active mate-
rial in following cycles. Therefore, our battery can be defined 
as an aluminum-manganese battery because both Al3+ and 
Mn2+ serve as charge carriers in the electrochemical reactions. 
The newly formed product was further examined by a series 
of characterizations. As seen from the STEM image and SAED 
pattern (Figure 4a), the broad and diffused halo rings indicate 
the amorphous nature of this material, in agreement with XRD 
results in Figure 3b. Elemental maps (Figure 4b–d) show that 
Al, Mn, and O elements exist and distribute uniformly in the 
material, implying that it consists of the three elements. The 
weak signal of Na in this product suggests the nonexistence 
of Na element in the charged product (Figure S19, Supporting 
Information). The ex situ XPS spectra (Figure  4e) show the 
Al 2p peak of the charged product shifted to a lower binding 
energy (74.9 eV) compared with the electrolyte (75.8 eV), indi-
cating that the Al signal in the EDS map and XPS are mainly 
from the charged product rather than the electrolyte adsor-
bents. It is noted that the Al 2p peak of AlxMnO2·nH2O has 
been reported to exhibit a close binding energy of 75.0 eV.[14] 
Besides, the Al 2p peak acquired at the fully discharged state 
may be attributed to the adsorption of residual Al3+ from the 
electrolyte when considering its close binding energy to the 
electrolyte.

As shown in Figure  5, the electrochemical reaction of the 
T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 cell involves three steps. In the first dis-
charge process (Figure 5a), the Bir-MnO2 cathode is reduced as 
soluble Mn2+ into the electrolyte, which has been confirmed by 
the XRD, XPS, and Raman spectra (Equation (1)). Simultane-
ously, the Al anode is oxidized as Al3+ ion to balance the charge 
(Equation  (2)). In the subsequent first charge process, Mn2+ 
ions in the electrolyte are oxidized to a higher valence state by 
combining with the Al3+ in the electrolyte to form the amor-
phous AlxMn(1−x)O2 on the cathode, accompanying with the 
plating of Al on the anode (Equations (3) and (4), Figure 5b). 
The reversible dissolution/deposition of Mn2+/AlxMn(1−x)O2 
redox reaction and the stripping/plating of Al take place 
in the following discharge/charge processes (Equation  (5), 
Figure  5c,d). To confirm the reaction mechanism of the 
T-Al/Bir-MnO2 battery (without MnSO4 additive), XRD, XPS, 
and Raman spectra of the ex situ samples have been carried out 
(Figure S20, Supporting Information). The results show that 
the variations of these spectra are in consistence with that of 
the T-Al/0.5Mn/Bir-MnO2 battery, illustrating the same reaction 
mechanism of the batteries with or without pre-adding MnSO4. 
Hence, the improved performance of aqueous AIBs by the Mn 
pre-addition strategy could be attributed to the increased Mn2+ 
for driving the formation of AlxMn(1−x)O2 during the charge pro-

3. Conclusion
In summary, a high-energy rechargeable aqueous aluminum-
manganese battery has been constructed by using a Birnes-
site manganese oxide as the pristine cathode, which can be 
significantly optimized by the pre-addition of Mn2+ to tune 
the electrochemistry in this system. With the pre-addition of 
0.5 m MnSO4, the aluminum-manganese battery can deliver a 
remarkably high capacity of 554 mAh g−1, an upper discharge 
voltage plateau of 1.35  V, and thus a high energy density of 
≈620 Wh kg−1 at a current density of 100 mA g−1 (based on 
the Bir-MnO2 material). The rate performance and cycle sta-
bility of the battery can also be greatly improved, including 
a capacity of 320 mAh g−1 maintaining for 65 cycles. By a 
series of characterizations on materials at different discharge/
charge states by techniques including ex situ XRD, ex situ 
Raman, XPS, TEM, and SSNMR, it was found that the pris-
tine Birnessite MnO2 cathode can be reduced to soluble Mn2+ 
ions during the first discharge. The subsequent charge of the 
battery leads to the formation of AlxMn(1−x)O2, which plays 
a role as the reversible cathode active material in following 
cycles. The pre-addition of Mn2+ such as MnSO4 can increase 
the concentration of Mn2+, and thus more efficiently shifts the 
reaction balance toward the formation of AlxMn(1−x)O2 during 
the charge processes, resulting in the observed enhancement 
of performance and stability of the battery. This study pro-
vides innovative insights into the development of high-energy, 
low-cost, and long-cycle aqueous AIBs toward prospective 
applications.

Bruker D8 Advance Diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). 
SEM was carried out by a Hitachi S-4800 instrument. TGA was 
carried out with a heating rate of 5 °C min−1 in N2 using a Labsys 
evo (France) analyzer. XPS was conducted using Thermo Scientific 
ESCALAB 250Xi spectrometer, equipped with monochomatized Al Kα 
X-rays (1486.7 eV). Raman spectra were obtained using a Renishaw 
analyzer (λ laser  = 532  nm). Transmission electron microscopy was 
obtained by using the FEI Themis Z. 27Al magic-angle spinning 
nuclear magnetic resonance (MAS NMR) experiments were carried 
out on a Bruker Avance III 500 MHz (11.7 T) spectrometer using a 
2.5 mm HX MAS NMR probe at resonance frequencies of 130.3 MHz 
and spun at a MAS rate of 25 kHz. Prior to the measurements, the 
samples were finely ground and packed into zirconia rotors. Then 27Al 
spectra were recorded after single-pulse π/6 excitation with repetition 
times of 1 s.
Electrochemical Measurements: The Bir-MnO2 cathodes were 
prepared by mixing Bir-MnO2 powder, Super P carbon (99%, Alfa 
Aesar Co., Ltd.), and polyvinylidene fluoride (99%, Alfa Aesar Co., 
Ltd.) in a weight ratio of 7:2:1 in N-methyl pyrrolidone (NMP, 99.9%, 
Aladdin Co., Ltd.) to produce a slurry, which was then coated on 
stainless steel foils. The cathode was further dried in a vacuum oven at 
80 °C overnight. The batteries were assembled using the as-prepared 
cathodes, Whatman Glass fiber as the separator, and aluminum foil 
as the anode in CR2032 coin cell. 2 m Al(OTF)3 in water with/without 
MnSO4 additive was used as electrolyte. The batteries were tested 
using Land Instrument (Wuhan LAND Electronics Co., Ltd.) at 25 °C. 
CV was carried out on Solartron electrochemical workstation. EIS 
test was performed on a CHI electrochemical workstation with an AC 
voltage of 5 mV amplitude in the frequency ranging from 100 kHz to 
0.1 Hz.
