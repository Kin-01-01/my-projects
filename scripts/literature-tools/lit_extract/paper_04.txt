Abstract 
Cathode materials of sodium-based batteries with high specific capacity and fast 
charge/discharge mode, as well as ultralong reversible cycles at wide applied temperatures, 
are essential for future development of advanced energy storage system. Developing 
transition metal selenides with intercalation features provides a new strategy for realizing the 
above cathode materials. Herein, we report a storage mechanism of sodium ion in hexagonal

1. Introduction 
Sodium based batteries have attracted much attention in the energy market as an 
alternative to lithium-based batteries, due to abundant and evenly distributed sources of 
sodium.[1] Compared to Sodium ion batteries (SIBs), Sodium metal batteries (SMBs) were 
proposed to further increase energy density considering the low redox potential (-2.7 V) and 
high theoretical capacity (1166 mAh g-1) of sodium metal anode.[1-3] As a rising star in the 
field of large-scale energy storage and power batteries, sodium metal batteries have the 
potential to expand its availability, but its development is limited by their interface stability 
and safety issues. For advance of special energy storage from wide temperature range 
adaptability to ultra-fast charge property, the research of high-performance sodium metal 
batteries is very necessary. To realize high energy density, good rate capability, and long 
cycling life of SMBs, cathode material become decisive. Cathode materials that are

low specific capacity. Traditional conversion materials (e.g., transition metal oxides and 
sulfides) have also been studied in SMBs, but the irreversibility of redox reaction and the

Recently, transition metal selenides have attracted attention for sodium storage in SIBs 
due to the high volumetric energy density, superior electrical conductivity (1×10-3 S m-1 for 
Se vs. 5×10-28 S m-1 for S) and excellent rate capacity.[9,10] Compared with oxygen and sulfur 
elements, the low electronegativity of selenium allows feasible modulated electronic structure 
and bonding state to alleviating the charge imbalance caused by ion insertion.[11,12] Classical 
metal oxide cathode materials for sodium ion batteries have difficulty maintaining a long 
cycling lifetime of high capacity due to their poor air stability and severe volume expansion, 
even though they have a high operation voltage and energy density. Similarly, sulfides with 
high capacity and high energy density for sodium-sulfur batteries have significant 
deficiencies in cycling performance and coulombic efficiency due to their own dissolution 
and the shuttle effect of sulfur. However, copper selenide like CuSe, as a typical example, 
with excellent electrochemical properties, may exhibit ion mobilities as high as Cu2Se

To address the above issue, in this work we have proposed a layered structure of 
hexagonal CuSe (h-CuSe) as the cathode of a novel SMB system. Based on the DFT 
calculation, h-CuSe consists of direct ion transport channels for faster ion diffusion. The 
storage behavior of sodium ions in h-CuSe can be described as the behavior of ion 
intercalation modulating Cu-Se structures by inducing adjustment of Cu-Se bonds to 
accommodate volume changes, which we define as an energy storage mechanism for 
intercalation induced localized conversion (ILC) reactions. In a word, sodium ions are 
preferentially induced to intercalate between interlayers, and followed by conversion 
reaction. Unlike traditional conversion reactions, the ILC mechanism allows rapid and 
sufficient interionic interactions. Upon sodium storage and release, the length of the Cu-Se 
bond near the surface and between layers varies, which alleviates the volume expansion,

2. Results and discussion 
Theoretical calculation of h-CuSe. The h-CuSe as an atypical layered structure material (the 
structure as shown in Figure 1a) consists of alternating CuSe3-Cu3Se-CuSe3 layers (Cu-Se 
layer) and Se-Se van der Waals (vdWs) layers along the z axis.[19] There are two Cu-Se layers 
and one intermediate Se-Se vdWs layer in one single crystal cell, in which the upper and 
lower Cu-Se layers are two multi-atomic layers with a same structure but different 
orientations. Since the Se-Se van der Waals layers in h-CuSe can provide anion sublattices to 
support fast ion channel of Na cations, it is expected that the h-CuSe possesses the ability of 
fast cation transport. As illustrated in Figure 1a, the diffusion of sodium ions in h-CuSe can 
lead to the formation of NaxCuSe intercalation structure, which can be divided into two types

Atomic and electronic structures of h-CuSe. The h-CuSe nanosheet was successfully 
synthesized by a simple one-step hydrothermal method. The single phase h-CuSe structure 
and P63/mmc space group of the prepared samples are confirmed through X-ray powder 
diffraction (Figure 2a). As shown in Figure 2a, the XRD patterns are consistent with the 
peak positions of standard CuSe (PDF#89-7391). The misfit peak at 45° derived from the 
(220) crystal plane of Cu2Se, indicating that there is a small amount of impurity phase in the 
sample. Notably, the peak at 31.06° corresponding to (006) plane of h-CuSe is the strongest 
diffraction peak instead of (102), which indicates that the growth orientation of h-CuSe is 
along the [100] direction.[22] Besides, inductively coupled plasma atomic emission 
spectrometry (ICP-OES) demonstrates the atomic ratio of Cu:Se to be close to 1:1 (50 at.% of 
Cu and 49 at.% of Se, Table S2). The lattice parameters of h-CuSe (a=3.942(3) Å, 
b=3.942(3) Å, c=17.245(5) Å, Table S3) were obtained by Rietveld refinement as shown in

Electrochemical characterization of h-CuSe. The electrochemical performance of h-CuSe 
was evaluated by using h-CuSe electrode as sodium metal battery cathode, and a copper mesh 
calendered sodium metal sheet was used as the anode[23] with ether based electrolyte (1M 
NaSO3CF3 in diglyme). The battery structure is shown in Figure S8. We use Cu foil with 
good conductivity as the current collector to speed up the charge transfer process. As shown 
in Figure S9, the use of Cu foil and Al foil shows similar electrochemical properties, 
indicating that the current collector has no serious impact on the sodium storage mechanism 
of h-CuSe. Figure 3a shows the typical cyclic voltammogram (CV) curves of first 5 cycles at 
0.1 mV s-1 within the voltage of 0.3 V-2.7 V. It can be found that four cathodic peaks are 
observed in the first cycle, which are around 1.8, 1.5, 1.0, 0.6 V, related to the intercalation 
process of Na+ into h-CuSe. Figure 3b demonstrates the charge and discharge (GCD) curves 
of the h-CuSe electrode cycling at current density of 0.25 A g-1 in voltage range of 0.3-2.7 V,

four discharging platform at around 1.8, 1.5, 1.0, and 0.6 V, which are consistent with the CV

3. Conclusion 
In summary, we synthesized a hexagonal CuSe combined with DFT calculation guidance 
and nanostructure engineering. We revealed the intercalation transport characteristics of 
sodium ions in the bulk phase of h-CuSe lead to localized conversion reaction and proposed 
the ILC reaction mechanism of sodium ions in h-CuSe for the first time. The h-CuSe based 
sodium metal batteries present excellent sodium storage (220 mAh g-1 at 3 A g-1 with over 
1000 cycles) and rate performance (250 mAh g-1 for 25 A g-1 and 126 mAh g-1 for 50 A g-1). 
By a series of electrochemical tests, ex-situ XRD, in-situ TEM and XAS, it is demonstrated 
that the intercalation regulates the local h-CuSe redox in the discharge process, promotes the 
change of Cu-Se bond structure to alleviate the effect of intercalation on volume expansion. 
The one-dimensional channel of fast ion transfer at the h-CuSe surface and interlayer 
indicated by molecular dynamics simulation allows sodium ions to exhibit higher rate 
capabilities in the bulk phase due to no diffusion limitation. Our work provides new 
understanding of the sodium storage mechanism of metal selenides and the ILC mechanism 
for achieving ultrafast charging capability with high energy density, which may be critical

4. Experimental section 
Material synthesis: 
The h-CuSe nanosheets were synthesized by one-step hydrothermal method. First, 2.0 
mmol CuCl2·H2O was dissolved in 30 mL deionized water to form a blue transparent 
solution. Then, 2 mmol Se powder, 2 mL hydrazine hydrate solution and 2 mL ethylene 
diamine were poured into a 50 mL Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave and ultrasonically 
dispersed for 10 minutes to evenly distribute selenium powder and form H2Se. Then, the 
obtained blue solution was transferred into the autoclave, where another ultrasonication 
process was carried for 10 minutes. Subsequently, the Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave 
was placed in the oven, and maintained at a temperature of 160 ℃ for 8 h. After the autoclave 
was cooled to RT, the precipitate was washed and collected centrifugally with ethanol and 
water 3 times, and then dried in the oven at 60 ℃ over 6 h.
