A B S T R A C T  
An ideal rechargeable battery will possess the merits of high specific capacity, long cycling stability, short 
charging time, high initial coulombic efficiency, wide working temperature range, and low cost. Sodium-ion 
batteries (SIBs) are expected as the next generation of energy storage devices, but achieving the above fea­
tures is still a major challenge. Herein, we have developed the 3D hierarchical FeSe2/rGO hybrids by a rational 
hydrothermal method for anodes in SIBs. The FeSe2/rGO hybrid SIBs exhibit high rate capacity (205.0 mAh g−1 
at 75 A g−1 vs 458.6 mAh g−1 at 0.5 A g−1), ultra-stable cycling life (417.7 mAh g−1 after 6000 cycles at 5 A g−1 
with a rather low decay rate of only 0.0006% per cycle), extremely high initial columbic efficiency (~98.6%), 
and excellent feasibility in a wide temperature range (–40℃ to 60℃). Besides, the FeSe2/rGO// Na3V2(PO4)3/C 
full cell delivers an energy density about 145 Wh kg−1 after 200 cycles at 0.15 A g−1 (286 W kg−1). The excellent 
electrochemical performances of FeSe2/rGO hybrid SIBs as an ideal rechargeable battery for the next-generation 
energy storage system.   
1. Introduction 
With the rapid development of modern society, energy storage de­
vices with high requirements are urgently needed for wide applications 
ranging from portable electronics, electric vehicles to industrial equip­
ment. To meet these huge demands, various electrochemical energy 
storage systems have been proposed, including supercapacitors (SCs) 
[1,2], lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) [3–5], sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) 
[6,7], metal-sulphur batteries [8,9], and metal-air batteries [10]. At 
present, only SCs and LIBs are widely commercially used. Although SCs 
have high power density, their energy density is quite low. LIBs also 
have obvious drawbacks such as long charging time, unavailability at 
low temperatures, and high cost of lithium. Naturally, an ideal 
rechargeable battery should possess the following merits: high specific 
capacity, long cycling stability, short charging time, high initial 
coulombic efficiency, and wide working temperature range, as well as 
low cost. Unfortunately, no battery system can meet all these criterions 
up to now. 
In recent years, SIBs have risen up as a feasible candidate for next- 
generation energy storage devices [11–13], considering the abundance 
of sodium reservoirs (2.36 × 104 mg/kg by the earth’s crust as the fourth 
most abundant element with uniform distribution) and the low cost of 
Na resources (~$200/t for Na2CO3 vs ~$10000/t for Li2CO3). However, 
the large ionic radius (1.02 Å) and molar mass (22.99 g/mol) of Na 
greatly affect its diffusion into the host materials. To resolve this prob­
lem, anode materials based on intercalation, alloying, and conversion 
reactions have been explored [14–18]. The traditional anodes such as 
graphite and graphene are not suitable to accommodate Na+ and

suffered from poor specific capacity. In recent few years, transition 
metal chalcogenides (TMCs) have been attracted consideration [19]. 
Transition metal selenides have higher electronic conductivity and 
faster reaction kinetics during conversion than their oxide and sulfide 
counterparts [20]. In particular, as a p-type semiconductor with narrow 
band gap (1.0 eV), iron selenide (FeSe2) is equipped with fascinating 
features such as fast electron transfer, high adsorption coefficient, strong 
quantum confined effect, and remarkable theoretical charge capacity 
[21,22]. Also, FeSe2 has advantages of low cost, abundance, and envi­
ronmental benignity, which has been paid much attention in SIBs most 
recently [23–25]. However, similar to other TMCs, FeSe2 is also suffered 
from the pulverization and serious structural degradation owing to the 
large volume changes occurring in conversion reactions with Na ions 
[26]. For the development of SIBs, the major bottleneck is to find suit­
able electrode materials with long cycling stability and superior rate 
capability. 
To overcome these issues, designing suitable microstructures of 
compounds is urgently needed and desirable. In this work, we elabo­
rately develop a strategy of 3D hierarchical FeSe2/rGO hetero- 
nanostructure hybrids anode for advanced SIBs. The FeSe2/rGO hy­
brids have the following characteristics: (1) it has a unique framework 
structure, where the rGO membrane forms a network and the FeSe2 
nanoparticles lives in the rGO “nest”; (2) the FeSe2 nanoparticles are 
comprised of smaller nanoparticles with secondary structure, which 
provides more active sites; (3) the FeSe2 and rGO combine tightly to 
form hetero-nanostructures. The unique structure offers fast transport 
channel of sodium ions, alleviates volume expansion during charge/ 
discharging processes and accelerates electrochemical reaction kinetics. 
In this work we have successfully established a rational hydrothermal 
approach to synthesize the state-of-the-art FeSe2/rGO hybrids. We 
evaluated FeSe2/rGO hybrids as an anode for SIBs, exhibiting high 
specific capacity, superior rate behaviors for ultra-fast charging, ultra- 
long cycling stability, extremely high initial coulombic efficiency, and 
long-term operation in a wide temperature range from −40℃ to 60℃. It 
is expected that the proposed strategy of 3D hierarchical FeSe2/rGO 
hybrids for SIBs can be as the next-generation energy storage system. 
2. Material and methods 
2.1. Synthesis of FeSe2/rGO hybrid 
In a typical procedure, 2 mmol FeSO4⋅7H2O was added to 20 mL 
distilled water. After fully dissolved, citric acid monohydrate was added 
to the solution under continuous stirring. Then, 4 mmol Na2SeO3 and 36 
mL distilled water were further introduced to form the mixed solution. 
After the mixture stirring to a clear solution, it was mixed with 4 mL of 
GO solution (5 mg mL−1) under magnetic stirring. After sonication for 
another 2 h, 8 mL of hydrazine hydrate (85 wt%) was added dropwise 
under vigorous stirring condition to produce the suspension. Finally, the 
suspension was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave 
(100 mL) to react for 12 h under the temperature of 140℃. After the 
reaction, the products were collected by centrifugation and successively 
washed with deionized water and absolute ethanol for 3 times, respec­
tively. After drying in vacuum at 60℃ for 12 h, the FeSe2/rGO hybrids 
were obtained. 
2.2. Materials characterization 
The crystal structures of hybrids were investigated through X-ray 
diffraction (XRD), (PANalytical, Netherlands, scan rate of 10℃ min−1) 
using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.540589 Å) at the Bede D1 system. Field- 
emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi S-4800) and 
energy-dispersive X-ray analysis spectroscopy (EDS) were used to 
analyze the morphology and element distribution of samples. The 
microstructure was examined by high-resolution transmission electron 
microscopy (HRTEM, TF20, Joel 2100F). X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS Supra. Kratos) measurements were executed to 
elucidate chemical states of elements. Thermogravimetric analysis 
(TGA) was performed using a Pyris 1 TGA in air at a heating rate of 20℃⋅ 
min−1. The specific surface area and pore structure of electrode mate­
rials were analyzed by the typical nitrogen adsorption/desorption 
isotherm and Barrett–Joyner–Halenda (BJH, ASAP2460) pore-size dis­
tribution. The Raman spectrum of samples was taken by Andor SR-500i- 
B1 Raman microscope. 
In situ XRD experiment during electrochemical process was per­
formed on a Bruker D8 Discover X-ray diffractometer with a non- 
monochromated Cu Kα X-ray source scanned at 2θ ranges of 
28.0–45.0◦. The in situ electrochemical experiment was conducted using 
the Nanofactory TEM–scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) holder in 
an FEI Tecnai F30 TEM. Microstructure evolution of the FeSe2/rGO 
hybrid anode during charge and discharge processes was detected by the 
TEM, and its phase changes were characterized by selected area electron 
diffraction (SAED) patterns. 
2.3. Assembly of sodium-ion batteries 
The electrochemical properties of the obtained FeSe2/rGO hybrids 
were analyzed using 2032-type coin cells. The sodium metal and glass 
fiber (GF/F) were used as the counter electrode and separator, respec­
tively. The electrolyte was a solution of 1 M NaCF3SO3 dissolved in 
diethyleneglycol dimethylether (DIGLYME). The anode was prepared by 
mixing the active materials, carbon black, and polyvinylidene fluoride 
(PVDF) binder in the solvent of N-methyl-pyrrolidinone in a weight ratio 
of 8:1:1. After grinding, the slurry was blade-coated onto the copper foil 
with the average mass loading of about 0.8 mg/cm2 and then dried at 
60℃ for 24 h. For full cell, the counter cathode was fabricated by 80 wt 
% of Na3V2(PO4)3/C (NVP/C), 10 wt% super p, 10 wt% PVDF on an 
aluminum foil with carbon. The mass ration of anode/cathode was aboot 
1:4.5. 1.0 M NaPF6 in DIGLYME was chosen as the electrolyte because 
both the anode of FeSe2/rGO and the cathode of NVP/C show good 
stability in this electrolyte. Meanwhile, to reduce the capacity loss of full 
cell, the anode and cathode were activated for 10 cycles before installing 
the full battery 
2.4. Electrochemical measurements 
The charge/discharge profiles of the obtained electrodes were 
investigated by cycling the cells in a potential range of 0.5–2.9 V at 
different current densities on CT2001A LAND battery test system. CV 
and EIS were measured on CHI760E electrochemical workstation (CH 
Instruments, Inc.). CV curves were measured at various scan rates from 
0.1 to 1.0 mV s−1. The EIS test was carried out in a frequency range of 
0.01 Hz to 100 kHz at certain potential. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Material characterization 
The FeSe2/rGO hybrids were rationally synthesized by a hydro­
thermal method. As manifested in Fig. 1a, the XRD patterns of FeSe2/ 
rGO are displayed, and all peaks match well with the standard patterns 
of orthorhombic FeSe2 (JCPDS Card No. 79-1892) in the space group of 
Pnnm. Three strongest peaks at 34.8◦, 36.2◦, and 48.2◦are corre­
sponding to the (111), (120), and (211) lattice planes of FeSe2, 
respectively. The peaks of rGO do not appear in the XRD patterns. The 
morphologies were characterized by SEM, as shown Fig. 1b and c. As 
demonstrated, the 3D hierarchical structure of FeSe2/rGO hybrids is 
rationally formed. The rGO membrane is arranged in a crisscross pattern 
to form a network structure with irregular compartments, acting as the 
framework. The FeSe2 microparticles (~1 μm) are composed of tightly 
aggregated smaller FeSe2 nanoparticles with the size of about 200 nm 
(Fig. 1b and c), which are fixed in the irregular compartment and

wrapped by the rGO membrane, just like a FeSe2 “bird” living in the rGO 
“nest”. The TEM image (Fig. 1d) further indicates the structure of rGO 
and aggregated FeSe2 nanoparticles. The HRTEM image (inset in Fig. 1d) 
shows the clear 2D lattice fringes with the distances of 0.37 nm and 0.24

The unique 3D hierarchical structure of FeSe2/rGO hybrid has 
evident merits for energy storage. Firstly, it can efficiently enhance the 
integrity of FeSe2 particles and buffer the volume expansion during the 
electrochemical reactions, which reinforces the structural stability for 
long-term cycle stability. Secondly, the interfaces between rGO and 
FeSe2 nanoparticles, as well as those among FeSe2 nanoparticles, can 
provide rich Na+ ion storage sites and fast channels for diffusion, leading 
to the extremely short-ion diffusion for accelerating Na+ ion migration 
and electron transfer. Thirdly, the rGO framework can improve the 
electrical conductivity of hybrid, which also accelerates the transfer of 
ions/electrons contributing to the high-rate capacity for sodium ion 
storage. We believed that the 3D hierarchical FeSe2/rGO hybrid pro­
vided in this work is a very suitable strategy for advanced energy 
storage. 
The FeSe2/rGO hybrids were further explored by XPS. The presence 
of the Fe, Se, C and O elements are detected in Fig. S1. The existence of O 
element may be due to the partial oxidation and adsorption of air on the 
surface of FeSe2 [21]. High-resolution of Fe 2p, Se 3d and C 1s spectra 
are shown in Fig. 2a–c. In Fig. 2a, distinct Fe 2p3/2 and Fe 2p1/2 doublets 
for both FeSe2 and iron oxides can be observed. There are two peaks at 
binding energies of 707.0 eV for Fe 2p3/2 and 719.8 eV for Fe 2p1/2, 
which are the characteristic peaks of Fe(II) in FeSe2[27,28]. Further­
more, peaks located at 710.9/712.3 eV and 724.9/727.0 eV correspond 
to Fe 2p3/2 and Fe 2p1/2 for the Fe 2+/Fe 3+ in the Fe2O3, respectively. 
The high reactivity of FeSe2 with oxygen causes its partial oxidation into 
iron oxides. The other peaks at 715 and 731 eV are ascribed to satellite 
peaks (sat.) [29]. In the Se 3d spectrum (Fig. 2b), the binding energies at 
54.5 eV for Se 3d5/2 and 55.4 eV for Se 3d3/2 are also confirmed to be in 
good agreement with the reported values for FeSe2 in literatures 
[28,30]. Additionally, the peak observed at 56.5 and 59.0 eV is related 
to Se–Se and Se–O, revealing the existence of metalloid Se and SeO2 
[31]. The C 1 s spectrum shown in Fig. 2c reveals the presence of carbon 
in sp2 C–C peak at 284.6 eV, indicating that GO is mostly reduced in the 
structure evolution process proposed above. 
The rGO content in FeSe2/rGO hybrids is 19.6%, as estimated from 
TGA curves (Fig. S2). Fig. 2d and e presents Raman spectrum of this 
sample. The peak of Se–Se stretching mode can be observed at 213.2 and 
278.8 cm−1, which may be attributed to Se–Se librational and stretching 
vibrations or their combination [27,32]. The peak at 388.7 cm−1 is 
probably caused by the quantity effect [27], because FeSe2/rGO hybrids 
are composed of many FeSe2 nanoparticles resulting in the high surface 
activity of the nanoparticles. In addition, the obvious D and G bands of 
rGO are observed in the 1000–2000 cm−1 range (Fig. 2e). The intensity 
ratio of the D band to the G band is about 2.69, indicating a large number 
of defects and the highly disordering degree of grapheme, which also 
confirms the reduction process from GO to rGO [32–34]. The specific 
surface area of FeSe2/rGO is calculated to be 6.68 m2 g−1 (much higher 
than that of pure FeSe2: 2.29 m2 g−1) based on the nitrogen isothermal 
adsorption–desorption curves (Fig. S3a). In addition, we also calculate 
that the specific surface area of rGO was 529.99 m2/g Fig. S3a). The 
Barrett–Joyner–Halenda (BJH) pore size distribution (Fig. S3b) shows 
the presence of a large number of mesoporous pores with an average 
diameter of 2.5 nm in the FeSe2/rGO hybrids, which can be used for Na +
ion storage and as channels for easy permeation of the electrolyte [29].

Fig. 3b shows the galvanostatic charge and discharge (GCD) curves 
of the FeSe2/rGO hybrid recorded in a range of 0.5–2.9 V at 0.5 A g−1. 
During the first sodiation process, an obvious voltage plateau is observed 
around 1.37 V. This is attributed to the slow reaction kinetics and the 
intercalation and conversion reactions that simultaneously occurred 
during the first discharge process [37]. During the second sodiation/ 
desodiation processes, GCD curves exhibit a slope-like tendency; a flat 
sodiation arc appears between 2.03 and 0.87 V and three gentle slashes 
between 1.01 and 2.26 V appear during the desodiation process. The 
change of voltage–capacity plateaus at first two cycles is consistent with 
the change of the redox peaks in the initial CV profiles (Fig. S4), which 
may correspond to side reactions and formation of SEI at the early stage. 
As the cycle progresses, the sodiation/desodiation platforms become 
more and more obvious (e.g., at 100th cycle). After 150 cycles, the 
sodiation/desodiation curves keep stable plateaus, which are located at 
1.45 and 1.82 V during the desodiation process and at 1.62, 1.10 and 
0.71 V during the sodiation process. The gradual increase in capacity 
before 100 cycles reveals that the long-term activation process may be 
necessary for the FeSe2/rGO hybrid system.

For practical applications under different climates, the electro­
chemical performances at high/low temperatures are very crucial to 
determine the feasibility of energy storage system, especially at low 
temperatures in the high latitude or high altitude areas [41,42]. In order 
to make the battery have a wide operating range and stable cycle per­
formance, we choose NaCF3SO3 and DIGLYME as conductive salts and 
electrolyte, respectively. This has been proved to be the very suitable 
electrolyte for anode material in SIB with fewer side reactions, less 
impedance, better charge transport and a stabilized surface passivation 
layer [43,44]. Then, we studied the performance of FeSe2/rGO hybrid 
SIBs in ether electrolyte at different temperatures (Fig. 4a). The des­
odiation capacity is up to 511.6 mAh g−1 at room temperature (RT, 
25℃) at 1 A g−1. The SIBs afford remarkable behaviors at low temper­
atures of 0, −10, and −20℃, and the desodiation capacity only shows a 
slight decrease, with more than 94.4%, 89.7% and 85.0% of the capacity 
at RT retained. Even at −30℃ and −40℃, it still has stable capacity of 
348.6 and 271.7 mAh g−1, which is 68.1% and 53.1% of the capacity at 
RT, respectively. Also, the SIBs still performs well at high temperatures, 
with almost the same desodiation capacity (~500 mAh g−1) at 40℃ and 
50℃ as those at RT. When the temperature is further increased to 60℃, 
the stable reversible capacity of 420.3 mAh g−1 is still found, which is 
82.1% of the capacity at RT. Meanwhile, the coulombic efficiency is 
always close to 100%, indicating that the battery can be fully charged 
and discharged between −40℃ and 60℃. 
The GCD curves at different temperatures (Fig. 4b) further confirm 
that the capacity is basically stable in the range from −40℃ to 60℃. 
With the temperature decreases, the charging platforms at ~1.5 and 
~1.75 V gradually move towards to high potential, while the dis­
charging platforms gradually move towards to low potential, revealing 
that the polarization of the battery is evident at low temperature. The 
high-temperature (60℃) long-cycle performance at 5 A g−1 is shown in 
Fig. 4c, delivering a stable nature with remaining reversible capacities of 
442.3 mAh g−1 after 200 cycles. Even at −40℃ (Fig. 4d), the battery still 
demonstrates a rather stable feature with a remaining capacity of 216.7 
mAh g−1 after 200 cycles. In conclusion, the sodium ion battery based on 
FeSe2/rGO hybrid anode has an exceptionally temperature adaptability, 
with high capacity retention at high and low temperatures. In particular, 
the battery has a fast transport rate at low temperatures, making its 
capacity retention as high as 53.1% at −40℃ as compared with the 
capacity at RT, which is much higher than that of lithium ion battery 
commercially used now. Naturally, the outstanding high/low tempera­
ture behaviors are ascribed to the unique 3D hierarchical hetero- 
nanostructure of FeSe2/rGO hybrids with characteristics of high con­
ductivity, fast ion transport, structural flexibility, and matrix activity. 
Our results firmly confirm that the FeSe2/rGO hybrid is very ideal for 
advanced all-climate SIBs. 
The excellent electrochemical performance of FeSe2/rGO//Na 
prompted us to further study its performance in full cells. NVP/C was 
chosen as the cathode material. Considering the very different properties 
of cathode and anode materials in different electrolytes, the electrolyte 
with 1.0 M NaPF6 in DIGLYME was selected for full cell. NVP/C cathode 
and FeSe2/rGO anode show stable cycling performance in this electro­
lyte, indicating that this electrolyte is a suitable electrolyte for the full 
cell (Fig. S6). Fig. 5a describes the cycling performances of FeSe2-rGO// 
NVP/C, where it delivers an energy density about 145 Wh kg−1 after 200 
cycles at 0.15 A g−1 (286 W kg−1) based on the mass of FeSe2/rGO and 
NVP. Compared with previous reports [45,46], the FeSe2/rGO//NVP/C 
full cell shows a higher energy density. Fig. 5b exhibits the charge and 
discharge curves of the FeSe2/rGO//NVP/C full cell with excessive 
NVP/C cycling between 0.3 and 3.5 V. During the cycling performance 
of FeSe2/rGO anode (Fig. 3a,b and e), the specific capacity and charging

To further study the dynamic changes during sodiation and des­
odiation, EIS data of the FeSe2/rGO anode after different cycling scans 
were recorded in Fig. 6c. It is obvious that all the EIS curves contain a 
semi-circle at high-frequency region and a sloping line with the inclining

to bounded diffusion space in active material, which is always observed 
in the thin film and nanoparticle battery electrodes [48]. According to 
previous report, the FeSe2/rGO nanoparticles may gradually pulverized 
into ultrafine nanoparticles after cycling, making it easier for Na+ to 
diffuse into the interior of particles [49]. Therefore, the Nyquist plots of 
FeSe2/rGO anode at the middle/high frequency are similar to the 
character of capacitors. Based on the previous literature [48,50–52], we 
selected the appropriate equivalent circuit to calculate the inner resis­
tance and charge transfer resistance (Table S3). As the cycle progresses, 
the inner resistance (Re) decreases from 7.58 Ω (1st) to 5.80 Ω (5th), and 
then gradually increases to 8.02 Ω (200th). At the same time, the elec­

showed a smaller Re (11.75 Ω) and Rct (16.10 Ω) than of pure FeSe2, 
indicating that rGO can enhance the electron transport and reduce the 
internal resistance (Fig. S8). Such low internal resistance and electron 
transport impedance indicate the excellent electrical conductivity of the 
electrode, resulting in excellent rate performance. According to the EIS 
curves, the fitted linear of Z’ with ω−1/2 and corresponding slope (σ, 
Warburg coefficient) and intercept were displayed in Fig. 6d. Then, 
based on the above Warburg coefficient and Eqs. (S4)–(S6), the DNa+
were calculated and exhibited in Table S4. As the cycles increase from 
1st to 200th cycle, the DNa+ increases from 2.02 × 10–14 to 2.89 × 10–13 
cm2 s−1. It implies that the Na+ can transport more and more easily with 
the cycling. It has been reported that the NaFeSe system is a kind of 
superconductor [53], being of ultrafast dynamics in both super­
conducting and normal states [54]. Previous study of iron diselenide 
thin films revealed that the presence of Na in FeSe2 benefits to the 
conductivity of the matrix and it is very easy for Na to diffuse in FeSe2 
[55]. Thus, this may give the explanation for the increasing capacity 
during deep activation and the excellent multiplier performance and 
temperature adaptability. 
In situ XRD was performed to investigate the intrinsic reaction 
mechanisms of the FeSe2/rGO electrodes. An in-situ cell was discharged 
to 0.5 V and then charged to 2.9 V. As shown in Fig. S9, during the initial 
sodiation process, there are four diffraction peaks of 29.3◦, 31.3◦, 34.9◦, 
and 36.3◦, which correspond to the (011) (101), (111), and (120) 
planes of FeSe2, respectively. While, the peaks located at 38.6◦is 
ascribed to the BeO [33]. As the discharging proceeds, the pattern of 
FeSe2 gradually becomes weaker, which doesn’t appear again in the 
XRD spectra when being charged. Also, the expected Fe and NaSe2 
products, as well as the possible intermediate products, are not observed 
in the discharged stage, implying the poor crystallinity nature of the 
products [33,56,57]. This may be ascribed to the tendency of amorphous 
state for the products, or that the products are ultrafine nanoparticles in 
cells, which cannot be well detected by XRD measurements. Therefore, 
the changes of elemental states (Se, Fe and Na) in the electrodes dis­
charged to 0.5 V and charged to 2.9 V were analyzed by XPS spectra 
(Fig. S10). After discharging to 0.5 V, the peak position of Se 3d and Se 
3p shifted to the right obviously (about 2 eV), and returned to the 
original position again after charging to 2.9 V. At the same time, an 
obvious Na 1s peak appeared in the electrode when the discharged to 
0.5 V. This indicates that the elemental state of Se does change when the 
discharge reaches 0.5 V, which may be caused by the generation of 
Na2Se. At the same time, the elemental Fe in the discharge product and 
the surface of FeSe2 is easily oxidized to Fe2O3 and Fe3O4, so no obvious 
change was observed. Regardless of the cases, they can both experience 
a high flexibility to minimize the structural stress during the following 
electrochemical processes [58,59], which may ensure the long-term 
cycling stability. Also, the insertion of Li+/Na+ ion can go through de­
fects readily [58], thus the poorly crystallized product with sufficient 
defects may have rapid diffusion rate and superior reversibility of the 
conversion reaction in charge/discharge processes. 
To gain further insight into the transformation process, the in-situ 
TEM measurements were carried out on the FeSe2/rGO hybrid elec­
trode with Na electrode. The structural features of the pristine FeSe2/ 
rGO hybrid and those in discharged process with increasing applied 
voltages are given in Fig. 7. The whole changing process can be found in 
Video S1. In Fig. 7a, the FeSe2 particles are attached tightly to rGO 
membrane forming hetero-nanostructures for the pristine material, with 
clear diffraction rings ascribed to FeSe2 in the SAED (Fig. 7b). When the 
Na electrode contacts with the FeSe2/rGO hybrid, there still is an 
obvious morphology change and volume expansion even without the 
applied voltage (Fig. S11), and the process is very fast, just within few 
seconds (~3 s). Naturally, this rapid bulk expansion is related to the 
diffusion of Na into the FeSe2 lattice and the fast diffusion rate of Na in 
the FeSe2 particle. This reveals that the sodium is very easy to diffuse 
within the hybrid electrode. When the applied voltage is −2 V, the 
volume expansion of FeSe2 particles becomes more evident (Fig. 7c), 
indicating that more and more Na is introduced under potential. The 
SAED pattern demonstrates that the discharged product has a series of 
rings of Na2Se and FeSe (Fig. 7d), indicating the intermediate formation 
of Na2Se and FeSe during the discharge process. When the applied 
voltage increases to −2.5 V, the volume of FeSe2 particles expands 
again, accompanied by a shunting of the contrast (Fig. 7e). Even the 
FeSe2 particles do not directly contact with Na electrode, they show 
significant volume changes, indicating that Na can diffuse through rGO 
rationally (also observed in Video S2). Meanwhile, the diffraction ring of 
Fe appears in the SAED diagram (Fig. 7f). When the applied voltage 
further increases to −3 V, the FeSe2 particles display obvious volume 
expansion again (Fig. 7g), meaning that the Na can be continuously 
inserted in the hybrid electrode. The diffraction rings of FeSe2 in the 
SAED of the discharged products disappear, and obvious diffraction 
rings of Na2Se and Fe appear, indicating that FeSe2/rGO has been fully 
discharged (Fig. 7h). In this case, we can find 3 phenomena: (1) the 
volume expansion of FeSe2 occurs in the entire region of the image, 
revealing that Na can rapidly diffuse between particles of FeSe2 with 
hundreds of nanometers or even microns, which is not limited within the 
range of several nanometers; (2) with the expansion of FeSe2 particles, 
they become bigger and bigger (may be composed of several broken 
smaller nanoparticles during the cycling) finally connecting with each 
other, but they are still located in their original sites due to the formation 
of tight hetero-nanostructures and the living in the rGO “nest”; (3) 
although the expansion of FeSe2 particles are violent, the expansion of 
the matrix, defined by the rGO framework, is limited, which ensures the 
structural stability and that the electrode expansion is not serious in 
practical.

205.0 mAh g −1 at an ultra-high 75 A g−1 (150C). Secondly, the elec­

rGO hybrid structure provides natural tunnels for sodium ion transport, 
considering the rather easiness of sodium ion migration in FeSe2 and the 
diffusion along the rGO express way, as well as the high conductivity of 
both materials. As the high-rate capacity comes from the fast ion 
diffusion in the bulk, the approach proposed in this work may be very 
applausive for practical applications of ideal SIBs. 
4. Conclusion 
In summary, FeSe2/rGO hybrids with unique framework structure 
have been prepared via one-step hydrothermal method. Combining the 
synergistic effects, the FeSe2/rGO hybrid anode for SIBs exhibits 
exceptional sodium storage behaviors in term of superior rate capability 
(205.0 mA h g −1 at 75 A g−1), as well as durable long-term cyclability at 
high current rate (remaining capacity of 417.7 mAh g−1 with a very low 
decay rate of only 0.0006% per cycle over 6000 cycles at 5 A g−1) and 
extremely high ICE (98.6%), outperforming the other reported anode 
materials for SIBs to date. More importantly, the FeSe2/rGO anode 
material can adapt to various temperatures and still maintain cyclic 
stability and considerable capacity retention (82.1% at 60℃ and 53.1% 
at −40℃). The corresponding full-cell FeSe2/rGO//NVP/C delivers a 
remarkable energy density about 145 Wh kg−1 after 200 cycles at 0.15 A 
g−1 (286 W kg−1). The remarkable electrochemical performance is 
ascribed to fast ion diffusion coefficient and specific structure evolution 
during sodiation and desodiation. Such a FeSe2/rGO anode with super- 
fast charging capacity and wide working temperature range promises to 
be a next-generation of energy storage devices.
