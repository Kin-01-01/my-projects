Intercalation Pseudocapacitance in 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx MXene
Hybrids for All-Climate and Long-Cycle Sodium-Ion Batteries
Zhenyun Zhao, Yang Wu, Rui Hu, Jianguo Lu,* Dongliang Chen, Tongtong Li,*
Yunna Guo, Liqiang Zhang,* Hongwen Chen, Zhizhen Ye, and Chuanfang (John) Zhang*
Layered transition metal dichalcogenides have great potential as anodes of
sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) due to their high theoretical speciﬁc capacity.
However, the restacking severely limits their accessible sites, leading to
undesirable speciﬁc capacity, cycle stability, and working temperature range.
Herein, a hierarchical 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx MXene hybrid is designed via a simple
liquid-mixing method, where VS2 is conﬁned in the conductive Ti3C2Tx matrix
with chemical connections built between them. The in situ transmission
electron microscopy analyses reveal that the hybrid depends on a very fast
and reversible intercalation/de-intercalation process between VS2 and NaxVS2
(where x = 1) to store sodium. Theoretical calculations disclose that the
Ti3C2Tx matrix remarkably enhances the charge transfer and alleviates the
volume expansion of VS2 especially after Na+ is inserted. Consequently, such
a rational design exhibits an intercalation pseudocapacitance-dominant
mechanism, with excellent speciﬁc capacity (522 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1), rate
capability (342 mAh g−1 at 10 A g−1), cycle life (116% after 3000 cycles), and
also all-climate workability (with high speciﬁc capacity and long-term cycle
stability even at 70 and −40 °C). This study may open up a new vision to
design fast-charging, long-cycle, and all-climate SIBs anodes based on the
intercalation pseudocapacitance.

Creating the van der Waals heterogeneous 2D structure is
an eﬀective method to enhance electrochemical properties of
VS2 nanomaterials.[11] MXene is a group of 2D layered transi-
tion metal carbide, nitride, or carbonitrides, with the formula of
Mn+1XnTx (M: transition metal element, X: carbon or nitrogen,
Tx: surface termination).[12] They are characterized by large inter-
layer spacing, high electrical conductivity, and excellent mechan-
ical ﬂexibility.[13] Several works have veriﬁed the decreased ad-
sorption energy and potential high capacity of the hybrids of VS2
with Ti2CO2, Ti2CS2, or V2CO2 MXenes as SIBs anodes through
theoretical calculations.[11,14] As a typical MXene, Ti3C2Tx has a
high theoretical capacity of 352 mAh g−1 for SIBs.[15] However,
there have been no works investigating the VS2/Ti3C2Tx MXene
hybrid as SIBs anodes so far, neither in theoretical nor practical
facets.
In this work, we design a hierarchical 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx MX-
ene hybrid, where the VS2 nanopetals are nicely conﬁned in the
conductive Ti3C2Tx microplates matrix with chemical bonds built
between them. We will demonstrate that it is possible to obtain
an ideal SIB anode with wide temperature range, high capac-
ity, long cycle, and high rate behaviors concurrently. The in situ
transmission electron microscopy (TEM), electrochemical kinet-
ics analyses, and ﬁrst-principles calculations reveal the interca-
lation pseudocapacitive mechanism between VS2 and NaxVS2,
notably where x = 1, which is an adsorption-controlled process
featured by very fast and reversible ionic insertion/de-insertion.
Besides, we ﬁnd the main limitations of VS2 are the slow Na+
insertion speed (not the de-insertion), remarkably decayed elec-
trical conductivity after Na+ insertion, and signiﬁcant volume ex-
pansion with Na+ inserted. However, combing with the conduc-
tive Ti3C2Tx matrix can well remedy the shortage of VS2. Con-
sequently, the design of 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx MXene hybrid shows
high speciﬁc capacity, fast-charging capability, long cycle, and all-
climate workability concurrently for advanced SIBs anodes.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Preparation and Design of VS2/Ti3C2Tx Hybrid
The VS2·NH3 precursor was synthesized via the solvothermal
reaction of NH4VO3 and thioacetamide (TAA) in ethylene gly-
col, which was annealed to obtain pure VS2. Figure 1a shows
X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) patterns of the VS2·NH3 precursor
and VS2. The diﬀraction peaks of the precursor correspond to
VS2·NH3 (JCPDF 41–0642), indicating that the NH3 produced by
TAA decomposition is inserted into VS2 to expand interplanar
spacing.[16] After annealing at 400 °C in an Ar atmosphere, the
peaks at 15.38°, 32.06°, 35.74°, 45.24°, 57.15°, 58.33°, and 59.56°
occur, assigned to the (001), (100), (011), (012), (110), (103), and
(111) planes of VS2 (JCPDF 89–1640), respectively. No VS2·NH3
peaks are observed after annealing, indicating NH3 is removed
from VS2 layers. In general, pure VS2 suﬀers from severe restack-
ing, as shown in Figure 1b, causing sluggish ionic transport,
limited accessible sites, and signiﬁcant volume expansion. Thus,
pure VS2-based SIBs anodes are diﬃcult to simultaneously ob-
tain high speciﬁc capacity, fast-charging capability, long cycle,
and all-climate workability.
Accordingly, this work introduces the Ti3C2Tx MXene as the
supporting framework of VS2 via a very simple liquid-phase mix-
ing method. Figure 1c brieﬂy describes the preparation of the

Figure 2j displays X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) sur-
vey scans of VS2, Ti3C2Tx, and VS2/Ti3C2Tx. Pure Ti3C2Tx con-

where O comes from slight surface oxidation, and C comes from
adsorbed air or carbonization of residual organic matter in its
precursor. The VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid contains all the elements
of Ti3C2Tx and VS2. Figure 2k–m displays high-resolution XPS
spectra of the VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid. The spectrum of Ti 2p is com-
posed of the Ti─C (455.9 and 461.9 eV), Ti─S (457.1 and 463.3
eV), and Ti─O bonds (459.0 and 464.4 eV). The Ti─S structure in-
dicates the S of VS2 forms chemical bonds with the Ti of Ti3C2Tx.
The spectrum of S 2p shows the S2−(161.6 and 162.6 eV), C─S─C
(164.0 and 165.2 eV), and C─SOx─C structures (169.1 eV). The
C─S─C structure is generated from the chemical combination
between Ti3C2Tx and VS2, and some of them are oxidized into
C─SOx─C. The spectrum of V 2p demonstrates the existence of
V─C (513.1 and 520.7 eV), V3+ (514.2 and 522.1 eV), and V4+

be from the side reduction, and the V─C bonds are mainly from
the chemical combination between Ti3C2Tx and VS2. The above
results indicate that the VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid is not just a physical
mixture, but chemical combination is built between them, which
can stabilize the heterogeneous 2D structure.
2.3. Electrochemical Performance
The CV curve of pure Ti3C2Tx (shown in Figure S5, Supporting
Information) exhibits no redox peaks, indicating a pseudocapac-
itive feature. Compared with it, VS2 and VS2/Ti3C2Tx both show
obvious redox peaks, as shown in their ﬁrst three CV cycles at
a slow scan rate of 0.2 mV s−1 (Figure 3a,b). The diﬀerence be-
tween the ﬁrst and the following cycles is mainly due to the for-
mation of solid electrolyte interface (SEI) ﬁlms. Moreover, pure
VS2 shows three reduction (or sodiation) peaks at 1.50, 0.75, and
0.4 V in the cathodic scan. The reduction peak at 1.50 V can be
ascribed to the insertion of Na+ into vacancies between diﬀerent
V─S layers.[6b,17] The reduction peaks below 1.50 V are attributed
to the phase transmission from VS2 to NaxVS2. In the anodic
scan, two oxidation (or desodiation) peaks at 1.58 and 2.12 V are
assigned to the de-insertion of Na+ and the reverse phase trans-
mission from NaxVS2 to VS2. The VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid (Figure 3b)
also contains these redox peaks, and it shows a new reduction
peak at 1.94 V maybe caused by some side-products generated
in the VS2–Ti3C2Tx mixing–annealing process. Comparing the
CV curves of VS2 and VS2/Ti3C2Tx, we can ﬁnd the hybrid owns
much larger response current but a less sharp shape, indicating
higher capacity and the pseudocapacitive feature. Besides, the
second and third cycles of the hybrid almost overlap, but those
of VS2 do not, indicating better stability and reversibility of the
hybrid.
Figure 3c shows discharge–charge curves of the VS2/Ti3C2Tx
hybrid at diﬀerent current densities, which have similar plateaus
voltages to redox peaks of CV curves and balanced charged and
discharged speciﬁc capacities. The discharge–charge curves of
pure VS2 are shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information, in
which the charged speciﬁc capacity is much larger than the dis-
charged one, mainly because of the mismatched desodiation and
sodiation kinetics, which will be discussed below. Figure 3d com-
pares rate performance of VS2, Ti3C2Tx, and VS2/Ti3C2Tx. Pure
Ti3C2Tx owns a very low capacity of just 12 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1.
In comparison, VS2 and VS2/Ti3C2Tx show much higher speciﬁc
capacities of 382 and 522 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1, respectively. De-
tailed speciﬁc capacities can be found in Table S1, Supporting
Information. The VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid exhibits excellent rate per-
formance with 342 mAh g−1 at an increased current density of
10 A g−1 and a higher capacity of 544 mAh g−1 than the original
value when the current density returns to 0.2 A g−1. However,
pure VS2 begins to decay at 10 A g−1 and shows a lower capacity
of 378 mAh g−1 when the current density returns to 0.2 A g−1,
indicating poorer rate performance than the hybrid.

demonstrate that the VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid can be applied in all-
climate conditions, and has applicational prospects in most hu-
man settlements or in some special environments.
2.4. Electrochemical Reaction Mechanism
To ﬁgure out the sodium storage mechanism of the VS2/Ti3C2Tx
hybrid, we carried out the in situ TEM characterization (with
the test system diagramed in Figure S13, Supporting Informa-
tion). Figure 4a–j shows the local morphological evolution of the
VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid during the ﬁrst two cycles. After the ﬁrst dis-
charge process (Figure 4a–c), the volume expands due to the in-
sertion of Na+. After the ﬁrst charge process (Figure 4d,e), the
volume shrinks due to the de-insertion of Na+. During the sec-
ond discharge/charge cycle (Figure 4f–j), volume expansion and
shrinking can also be observed, which indicate Na+ can reversibly
insert to and de-insert from the hybrid (recorded in Video S1,
Supporting Information).
To study the phase transmission in discharge/charge cycles,
the SAED patterns of the I, II, III, and IV areas (marked in
Figure 4a,c,e,h) are shown in Figure 4k–n, which are correspon-
sive to the initial, ﬁrst discharged, ﬁrst charged, and second dis-
charged states, respectively. When the material is in the initial
state without charging or discharging (Figure 4k), its diﬀrac-
tion spots are assigned to the (011), (012), (103), and (202)
planes of VS2 (JCPDF 89–1640). After the ﬁrst discharge process
(Figure 4l), the diﬀraction spots are attributed to the (104), (018),
and (024) planes of NaVS2 (JCPDF 03-065-3668). This proves that
Na+ insert into VS2 to form NaVS2, as shown below.
VS2 + Na+ + e−→NaVS2
(1)
After the ﬁrst charge process (Figure 4m), the diﬀraction spots
correspond to VS2 again, indicating that Na+ smoothly de-insert
from the hybrid, and the reverse reaction occurs, as shown below.
NaVS2 →VS2 + Na+ + e−
(2)
After the hybrid is discharged for the second time (Figure 4n),
the insertion of Na+ recurs, and the diﬀraction spots of NaVS2 are
observed again. To the best of our knowledge, it is the ﬁrst time
that in situ TEM is used to disclose the sodium storage process of
VS2-based compounds. The intercalation reaction between VS2
and NaVS2 is essentially caused by the highly reversible insertion
and de-insertion of Na+ at vacancies among VS2 layers, as shown
in Figure S14, Supporting Information.
According to previous literatures, the VS2-based nanomateri-
als mainly depend on the conversion and intercalation reactions
to store charges. Chen et al.[6a] have reported that both reactions
can be described by VS2 →NaxVS2, where S will be reduced when
0 < x ≤1, and V and S will be reduced when 1 < x ≤2, but the
conversion reaction to generate V and Na2S would occur when
x = 3. Larger x values mean more inserted Na+ in a unit and
larger volume expansion. Thus, the conversion reaction usually
results in large speciﬁc capacity but short cycle life. To avoid
the conversion reaction, most works ensure the voltage window
above 0.3 V. Even so, the two reactions may still coexist, that is,
the spherical VS2 nanosheets (329 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1 with a
retention of 82.95% after 800 cycles) [8] and the 3D rose-like VS2
(571 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1 with a retention of 98.95% after 350
cycles).[18] We speculate this is because sluggish ionic kinetics
make ions accumulate and then excessively insert to very local
spaces.
Among the works focusing on the intercalation mechanism,
some did not characterize the x value in NaxVS2,[19] and some
found x > 1 for their speciﬁc VS2 nanomaterials (i.e., the hier-
archical ﬂower-like VS2 with x = 2, showing ≈600 mAh g−1 at
0.1 A g−1 which retained 83% after 700 cycles in 0.3≈3 V[17]). Be-
sides, there are two works with x = 1, that is, the layer-by-layer
stacked VS2 nanosheets with 250 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1 which re-
tained ≈100% after 600 cycles in 0.4–2.2 V,[20] and the nano-grain
dependent 3D hierarchical VS2 microrods with 310 mAh g−1 at
0.2 A g−1 which retained ≈105% after 270 cycles in 0.5–3 V.[21]

3. Conclusion
This work designs a hierarchical 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx MXene hybrid,
in which VS2 is conﬁned in the conductive Ti3C2Tx matrix with
chemical connections built between them via a simple liquid-
phase mixing method. Pure VS2 suﬀers from the restacking, re-
sulting in limited accessible sites, sluggish Na+ transport chan-
nels, and signiﬁcant volume expansion. The Ti3C2Tx MXene ma-
trix not only improves active sites but also provides a large quan-
tity of smooth ionic transport channels. The in situ TEM indicates
the hybrid relies on the very fast and reversible intercalation/de-
intercalation process between VS2 and NaxVS2, where x = 1, to
store sodium, which is intrinsically featured by fast ionic trans-
ports and small volume expansion. This leads to an intercala-
tion pseudocapacitance-dominant mechanism, and the Ti3C2Tx
matrix can further buﬀer the volume expansion, so the hybrid
achieves excellent speciﬁc capacities, rate capabilities, and cy-
cle stability even at extreme temperatures concurrently. Besides,
we found the sodium storage of pure VS2 is mainly limited by
the Na+ insertion step (not the de-insertion), and their electrical
conductivity would remarkably decrease after the Na+ insertion.
However, the Ti3C2Tx matrix can well remedy the disadvantages,
which were veriﬁed by theoretical calculations. In summary, VS2
and Ti3C2Tx are a perfect match, and the proposed simple liquid-
phase mixing method can chemically combine them, contribut-
ing to the intercalation pseudocapacitance-dominant behavior.
This study is expected to open a new version for designing ad-
vanced SIBs.
4. Experimental Section
Preparation of Ti3C2Tx Dispersion:
Ti3C2Tx is a 2D multilayer material
from etching the Al layers in raw Ti3AlC2. 2 g lithium ﬂuoride (LiF) was im-
mersed into 40 mL 9 m hydrochloric acid (HCl). The mixture was stirred for
1 h until LiF was completely dissolved. Then, 2 g Ti3AlC2 (Jilin 11 Technol-
ogy Co., Ltd) was added and stirred at 36 °C for 24 h. The reaction product
was centrifuged at 5000 rpm and washed several times until PH of the su-
pernatant got neutral, during which the bottom Ti3C2Tx was observed to be
gradually expanded. Then, the dark green Ti3C2Tx dispersion was obtained
through ultrasound (30 min, 100 W) and centrifugation (8000 rpm, 5 min)
treatment, and such a process was repeated for several times, ﬁnally ob-
taining the Ti3C2Tx dispersion with a concentration of ≈2 mg mL−1.
Preparation
of
VS2·NH3:
2
mmol
ammonium
metavanadate
(NH4VO3) was added into 30 mL ethylene glycol and then stirred at
60 °C for 1 h to obtain an orange solution. After cooling, the above
solution turned yellow, and 10 mmol TAA and 5 mmol urea were added
and stirred for 30 min. Then, the mixed solution was transferred into a 50
mL Teﬂon-lined autoclave and kept at 180 °C for 20 h. After cooling, the
as-prepared VS2·NH3 was collected, washed with ethyl alcohol for three
times, and vacuum-dried at 60 °C for 12 h.
Synthesis of 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx Hybrid:
A simple liquid-phase mixing
method was used to synthesize the 2D VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid. The VS2·NH3
precursor (240 mg) was ultrasonicated in 40 mL ethanol for 1 h before
added to the Ti3C2Tx dispersion (solid mass: 60 mg) and stirred for 1 h.
In this process, no precipitation was observed. The product was vacuum
ﬁltered and dried at 60 °C for 12 h to obtain the VS2·NH3/Ti3C2Tx hybrid.
Subsequently, the above hybrid was annealed at 400 °C under ﬂowing ar-
gon for 1 h to remove NH3. The pure VS2 and pure Ti3C2Tx samples did
not need the mixing step and were directly obtained by annealing.
Characterizations:
XRD (Rmpyrean, PANalytical B.V., Netherlands) us-
ing Cu K𝛼radiation (𝜆= 1.540598 Å) was used to investigate crystal struc-
tures. Morphologies were scanned by HRTEM (JEM-2100, JEOL, Japan),
and SEM (S-4800, Hitachi, Japan). EDX in conjunction with SEM and TEM
was used to analyze the elemental composition and distribution. XPS (ES-
CALAB250 Xi, Thermo, America) was used to evaluate elemental valences.
Besides, BET and BJH were utilized to study the speciﬁc surface area and
pore size distributions (BET/BJH, ASAP2020, Micromeritics, America).
Battery Assembly:
Electrochemical measurements were carried out
based on the assembled 2032-type coin cells. The sodium sheet was used
as the counter and reference electrode. The working electrode was pre-
pared by mixing the 70 wt% speciﬁc active materials (pure VS2, pure
Ti3C2Tx and VS2/Ti3C2Tx hybrid), 20 wt% super-P and 10 wt% polyvinyli-
dene ﬂuoride (PVDF) in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The active mate-
rials, super-P, PVDF, and NMP were used as electrode active materials,
conductive agent, binder, and solvent, respectively. After grinding, the ho-
mogeneous slurry was blade-coated on the copper foil and dried overnight
at 60 °C. The average mass loading of the active materials was ≈1.3 mg
cm−2. The glass microﬁber (Whatman GF/F) was used as the separator,
and 1 m NaCF3SO3 dissolved in diglyme was used as the electrolyte (with
the reasons detailed in Note S3, Supporting Information). All the cells were
assembled in an argon-ﬁlled glovebox with lower oxygen and water than
0.1 ppm.
Electrochemical Measurement:
The sodium half-cells were cycled in the
range of 0.3–3.0 V at diﬀerent current densities through the CT2001A
LAND battery test system to explore electrochemical properties. Cyclic
voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)
were tested on a CHI760E electrochemical workstation (CH Instru-
ments, Inc.). CV was carried out in the potential range of 0.3–3.0 V at
the scan rates of 0.2–1.0 mV s−1. The frequencies of EIS were from
100 kHz to 0.01 Hz under the AC voltage amplitude of 5 mV. Besides,
in situ TEM was carried out to study the reaction mechanism by Titan G2
ETEM.
